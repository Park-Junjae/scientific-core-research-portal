# Assembly-state gating

## Abstract

This demonstration hypothesis treats RNA protection as a **time- and state-dependent gate**. The key question is not whether an RNA element raises total abundance, but whether it selectively preserves a productive molecular state during the interval when the editing complex is assembled.

## Causal mechanism

The proposed gate changes the residence-time distribution of intact RNA near the productive complex. If that distribution is causal, protection should alter product formation more strongly than it alters mature bulk abundance. The hypothesis therefore predicts a separable relationship between total RNA, assembly-proximal RNA, and final product.

## Evidence boundary

The synthetic example assumes only component-level precedent for structured RNA protection. It does not claim that the complete architecture has been experimentally demonstrated. A valid test must distinguish protection of the relevant state from a generic expression increase.

## Decisive experiment

Compare matched constructs that preserve total expression while changing the timing or assembly dependence of protection. Measure bulk abundance, a proximal-state readout, intended product, byproducts, and cellular burden in the same experimental series.

## Result patterns

- **Supportive:** productive-state exposure and intended product rise together without a proportional rise in bulk RNA.
- **Unsupportive:** final product follows bulk abundance alone or protection increases burden without improving product purity.

## Kill criterion

End this hypothesis if the state-selective contrast cannot be separated from generic RNA stabilization or if the intervention worsens product quality at matched activity.
